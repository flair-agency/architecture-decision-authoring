## Review finding disposition: source revision and authenticity

The separate Code Review finding is preserved at [PR #52 review thread](https://github.com/flair-agency/architecture-decision-authoring/pull/52#discussion_r4156101264). It treats the Issue #72 `updated_at` time as evidence that the issue body changed after the owner outcome and requests non-consumable export or a further owner authorization for an immutable source snapshot.

The public Issue #72 record shows `created_at` `2026-09-24T06:47:00Z`, `updated_at` and `closed_at` both `2026-09-25T09:04:18Z`. Its public timeline contains the owner outcome comment at `2026-09-24T06:57:17Z`, a progress comment at `2026-09-25T09:04:17Z`, and closure at `2026-09-25T09:04:18Z`; it contains no `edited` event. The currently served issue body has SHA-256 `7d045342f1cd6707f1ca0679d864608f93e2664d7c25e8d3d4175e5d8ef53296`, matching the preserved proposal snapshot. These observations show that the later `updated_at` timestamp coincides with the later comment and closure, not a recorded body-edit event. Absence of an edit event and a matching current body do not prove what bytes were present at the historical outcome time; the original issue body still has no immutable Git revision, so source/owner authenticity remains `not-verified`.

The adopted product contract says source mapping preserves provenance but does not certify a source as authentic or authoritative (`docs/architecture.md:24–38`, “Input classification and source mapping”). It requires the package to bind the Proposal bytes to a locally available placement commit; [decision record 0004](../../../docs/decisions/0004-bind-proposal-revision-to-committed-bytes.md) explicitly says this is a structural integrity check and does not authenticate the repository, owner, or authorization evidence. The historical owner comment, its unique option discriminator, and its explicit reference to contemporaneous `de7d664` identify the selected option in context; the placement commit anchors the preserved snapshot bytes for this trial. The current records state both limitations without claiming historical authenticity.

Accordingly, the demand to authenticate the original Issue body or obtain a new owner authorization is not a requirement of the adopted product contract. The finding's underlying authenticity limitation remains disclosed, rather than converted into a new approval criterion. The historical `Adopt` outcome, proposal snapshot, member, placement anchor, and package bytes are unchanged; this is not a new adoption or `Amend`.
